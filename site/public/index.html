A licence is not a job

Everyone downloads the sponsor register, sees a hundred thousand companies, and starts mass-applying. Put the register next to the actual visa grants and the arithmetic is blunt.

121,929
organisations licensed to sponsor Skilled Workers
÷
86,750
new sponsored hires in 2025, from overseas and from inside the UK
=
0.71
sponsored hires per licensed sponsor, per year
And that is the mean. A few hundred NHS trusts, consultancies and banks sponsor dozens each, so the median licensed sponsor sponsors nobody at all in a given year. The register tells you who may sponsor. It never tells you who does.

Newly licensed

There is no official archive of the register — the asset URL changes on every publish and nobody publishes history. This site keeps its own daily snapshots and diffs them. A company that has just paid for a licence is a company that intends to use it, which makes this the highest-signal list in the whole dataset.

What this data cannot tell you

  • No per-sponsor volumes exist. The certificate-of-sponsorship datasets were discontinued after year-ending December 2024 and were only ever aggregate. Nobody publishes how many people a named company sponsored. Any product showing you a per-company count is guessing.
  • A licence says capability, not intent. A company can hold one for years and never use it.
  • The register carries no company number — only name and town — so matching it to Companies House is inherently fuzzy.

Removed is not revoked

When a sponsor disappears from the register it can mean a voluntary surrender, an expiry, a merger, or simply a rename. The Home Office publishes no revocations list for the worker routes — only aggregate counts of action taken against sponsors.

So departures are labelled removed from register here and nowhere is a revocation implied. Stating otherwise about a named company would be defamatory if wrong.
